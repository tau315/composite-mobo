\section{Overview}

This suite evaluates \emph{composite} multi-objective Bayesian optimization
(MOBO): instead of fitting a surrogate directly to the objective vector
$f(x)\in\mathbb{R}^M$, the surrogate models an intermediate \emph{raw
response} $g(x)\in\mathbb{R}^K$ that a real simulator or solver computes, and
a \emph{known}, closed-form reduction $L$ recovers the objectives,
\[
    x \;\longrightarrow\; g(x) \;\longrightarrow\; f(x) = L\big(g(x)\big).
\]
The composite variant of each solver fits $K$ per-component surrogates to
$g$ and applies $L$ analytically; the direct variant fits $M$ surrogates to
$f$. Every benchmark below is a controlled A/B: identical objective, Pareto
front, and evaluation budget; only the surrogate target differs.

\paragraph{Solvers.} Each benchmark is run through up to four solver families,
each in a direct and a composite variant:
\texttt{standard\_mobo} (qEHVI), \texttt{chebyshev\_bo} (augmented-Chebyshev
scalarization), \texttt{spherical\_chebyshev\_bo} (a spherical scalarization
for high dimension), and \texttt{batched\_morbo} (trust-region MORBO).

\paragraph{Suite gating.} ``Low''-suite benchmarks ($\lesssim 10$D) run
\texttt{standard\_mobo}/\texttt{chebyshev\_bo}/\texttt{batched\_morbo};
``high''-suite benchmarks run \texttt{spherical\_chebyshev\_bo}/
\texttt{batched\_morbo} (plain-kernel scalarization is a weak test at high
dimension). Reported numbers are the composite-minus-direct percentage change
in final dominated hypervolume, paired per seed, at $n$ seeds; $*$ marks
$p<0.05$ (paired $t$-test).

\section{Benchmarks and their composite structure}

\subsection{Synthetic composite functions}
These have a decomposable objective by construction and serve as mechanism
probes.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{DTLZ2} ($6$D/$2$obj, $100$D/$2$obj, $600$D/$2$obj; also a
  $6$D/$5$obj variant). Objective $f_m = (1+g)\prod\cos(\cdot)$ with
  $g=\sum_j (x_j-0.5)^2$; the raw response exposes the per-variable distance
  terms, so $g$ is a sum of one-dimensional components. The canonical
  decoupled case.
  \item \textbf{Ackley--Griewank} ($6$D, $50$D / $2$obj),
  \textbf{Five-Ackley} ($6$D/$5$obj), \textbf{Langermann--Ackley}
  ($6$D/$2$obj), \textbf{Projected Langermann} ($500$D/$2$obj): additive
  synthetic multi-objective landscapes whose raw response exposes the
  additive terms feeding each objective.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Real-world benchmarks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Summit SnAr reactor} ($4$D/$2$obj). Nucleophilic aromatic
  substitution in a plug-flow reactor (Felton et al. 2021; Hone et al. 2017
  kinetics). Raw response: the five outlet species concentrations plus the
  product molar flow; reduction: space-time yield (linear in the product
  flow) and E-factor (a nonlinear \emph{ratio} of species masses).
  \item \textbf{Nanoparticle RGB} ($6$D/$3$obj). Mie-scattering multilayer
  nanoparticle designed for RGB-selective structural color; raw response is
  the per-wavelength scattering response reduced to three color objectives.
  \item \textbf{CORT TG119 radiotherapy} ($418$D/$3$obj). Intensity-modulated
  radiotherapy dose optimization on the TG119 phantom; the raw response is
  the per-voxel dose, reduced to target-coverage / organ-sparing objectives.
  \item \textbf{Penicillin fermentation} ($7$D/$3$obj). Fed-batch bioreactor;
  raw response is a checkpointed fermentation trajectory reduced to yield,
  CO$_2$, and time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Power-flow benchmarks (MOBO-HighDim contribution)}
Real IEEE 14-bus optimal power flow from the CEC2021 RWCMOP suite. The raw
response is the per-bus power injection vector, computed by a single
admittance solve $I=YV,\ S=V\odot\bar I$; because the admittance matrix is
sparse, each per-bus quantity depends only on its local neighbors, and the
objectives are decoupled sums over them.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RCM40} ($34$D/$2$obj): active/reactive power loss.
  \item \textbf{RCM46} ($34$D/$4$obj): fuel cost, active loss, reactive loss,
  voltage deviation.
  \item \textbf{MOOPF-5} ($34$D/$5$obj): RCM46's four objectives plus the
  Kessel--Glavitsch L-index voltage-stability margin. A \emph{constructed}
  benchmark (not a CEC2021 case, which max out at RCM46's four objectives),
  built to test whether more decoupled objectives amplify the composite
  advantage.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Controlled decoupling benchmark}
\textbf{Blocked DTLZ2} (\texttt{benchmark\_dtlz2\_blocked\_reference.py}).
DTLZ2 with the objective, input dimension, and objective count held fixed
while the distance term $g$ is partitioned into $K=\lceil (d{-}1)/b\rceil$
contiguous blocks; each raw component is a partial sum over $b$ inputs.
Because $\sum_i \text{block}_i = g$ for every $b$, the objective is identical
across the sweep -- only the effective input-dimension of the raw components
($b/d$) varies, isolating decoupling from all other factors.

\section{Key findings}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The largest wins are on decoupled power-flow problems, and
  scale with objective count.} On the identical IEEE 14-bus system the
  composite advantage grows RCM40 ($+0.8\%$, 2 obj) $\to$ RCM46 ($+5.4\%$,
  4 obj) $\to$ MOOPF-5 ($+23.5\%$, 5 obj). Each added decoupled objective
  handicaps direct modeling (more coupled surrogates) more than composite.

  \item \textbf{Method dependence is real.} CORT TG119 flips sign by solver:
  composite \emph{loses} $-2.0\%$ on \texttt{batched\_morbo} but \emph{wins}
  $+1.5\%$ on \texttt{spherical\_chebyshev\_bo}. The composite advantage is
  strongest with trust-region acquisition and weakest with plain
  scalarization.

  \item \textbf{Chemistry / trajectory benchmarks do not benefit.} SnAr
  ($\approx$tie), Penicillin ($-2.5\%$), and CORT-on-morbo are cases where the
  raw response is one globally-coupled state (a reaction/fermentation
  trajectory, a global dose solve); the intermediates are no easier to model
  than the objectives.

  \item \textbf{Decoupling, not reduction nonlinearity, is the discriminator.}
  RCM46 wins with a purely \emph{linear} reduction (sums), while SnAr's
  \emph{nonlinear} E-factor ratio still ties. Across the suite, the effective
  input-dimension of the raw response separates wins from losses.

  \item \textbf{Causal confirmation (blocked DTLZ2).} Holding the objective,
  dimension, and objective count fixed and varying only the raw-response block
  size, the composite advantage falls monotonically from $+11.9\%$ (fully
  decoupled) to $+7.4\%$ (fully coupled), Pearson $r=-0.997$
  (Figure in \texttt{ablation\_results/\_decoupling\_sweep/}). Decoupling
  \emph{causes} the advantage; it is not merely correlated with it.
\end{enumerate}
